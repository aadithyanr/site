# first time in blr

i grew up in dubai. before this, i had been to india twice.

so blr was not just my first time in the city. it felt like my first real time in india.

dubai makes life very legible. roads make sense. timings mean something. you can move from one air conditioned box to another without the city asking much of you.

blr makes you earn context.

google maps says twenty five minutes and everyone understands this is just a theory. plans move. rain appears. an auto ride becomes a small negotiation. somehow the day still works.

i came expecting the traffic. i did not expect how quickly the city would pull me in.

## cracked people everywhere

the first thing that got me was the density of cracked people.

not just impressive people on paper. people building weird, specific things with very high agency. people who ship before they have the vocabulary to make what they are doing sound important.

conversations start one level deeper here. nobody spends forty minutes explaining that ai is going to be big. they show you the thing they built last night and ask why your version is not live yet.

i love that.

online, you can usually find an answer. in blr, you meet someone who gives you a better question. that is much more valuable.

there is also very little ceremony around ambition. someone will casually tell you about the company they are building, the research they are doing, or the product they just killed. then they will ask if you want chai.

it makes building feel normal. not easy, just normal.

## everyone knows someone

blr rewards showing up.

you meet one person for coffee. they add you to a group. that group leads to dinner. at dinner you meet someone you were supposed to email six months ago. the next morning you are sitting together fixing something.

there is no clean directory for this. the city reveals itself through people.

what surprised me most was how generous everyone was with context. intros were quick. doors were open. people shared what they knew without turning every conversation into a transaction.

for someone new to the city, that matters. i did not need to understand all of blr on day one. i just needed a few good people who were willing to pull me into their corner of it.

the energy is not one giant event. it is a hundred small moments. a founder working from the next table. a conversation that changes what you build that week. a dinner that runs late because everyone is still arguing about product.

spend enough time around that and your own clock gets faster.

## the food is ridiculous

also, the food.

mai mai became an easy favourite. japanese leaning food, really good cocktails, and the kind of room where dinner can stretch without anyone checking the time.

smash guys does burgers and fries exactly how i want them. messy, direct and no unnecessary philosophy.

highstreet blr is great for chai and south indian food. it is the sort of place that fixes a long day very quickly.

naru is worth the effort when you can get a seat. kopitiam lah is for breakfast that quietly becomes two hours. bar spirit forward is for one proper drink.

i ate better here than i expected to, which is saying a lot because i planned half my days around food.

[[media:food]]

## i get it now

blr is not an easy city to romanticise while your cab has moved six hundred metres in twenty minutes.

then you have one of those days.

you meet three brilliant people. someone makes an intro that changes your week. you eat extremely well. you get home with more ideas than you left with.

that happened often enough for the friction to stop feeling like the whole story.

i came in knowing almost nobody. now i understand why so many people choose to build here.

blr, i get it now.
